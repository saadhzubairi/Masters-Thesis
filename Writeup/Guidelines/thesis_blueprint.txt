  - State clearly: "We propose LBEADS-NET, an algorithm-unrolled neural

  - Why baseline matters for quantification: peak area integration,
    limit of detection, calibration curves
  - Peak shapes: Exponentially Modified Gaussian (EMG) model as the gold
    standard for chromatographic peaks (Grushka, 1972; Naish & Hartwell, 1988)
    — relevant because our synthetic data and evaluation use this model
  - Include figure: annotated chromatogram labeling peaks, baseline, noise

SECTION 2.2: Classical Baseline Correction Methods (~6–8 pages)

  2.2.1: BEADS — Baseline Estimation And Denoising using Sparsity (~4–5 pages)
    THIS IS THE CORE — cover in full mathematical detail:

    The optimization problem:
      minimize  ||Dx||_1 + lambda_0*||x||_1 + (lambda_1/2)*||Hf||_2^2
                + (lambda_2/2)*||f||_2^2
      subject to  y = x + f  (+ noise tolerance)

    Variable splitting / MM derivation (high level):
    - Majorization-Minimization for non-smooth L1 terms
    - Iterative reweighting: w(z) = 1/(|z| + eps)

    The BAfilt construction:
    - Band-pass filter via cascaded Butterworth sections
    - scipy.signal.butter -> banded Toeplitz matrices A and B
    - Role of filter order d and cutoff frequency fc

    The iterative update scheme:
    . f-update: banded linear solve
      (B^T B + lambda_1 H^T H + lambda_2 I) f = B^T(y - x)
    . x-update: proximal/shrinkage with asymmetric penalty
      Theta weighting: how r controls asymmetry
    . Reweighting step: update w(z) = 1/(|z| + eps)

    Role of each parameter:
    | Parameter | Controls                          | Range      |
    |-----------|-----------------------------------|------------|
    | lambda_0  | Peak sparsity (L1 weight)         | 0.001-1.0  |
    | lambda_1  | Baseline smoothness (1st deriv)    | 0.01-10    |
    | lambda_2  | Baseline fidelity (2nd deriv)      | 0.01-10    |
    | r         | Asymmetry ratio                   | 1-10       |
    | fc        | Cutoff frequency (band-pass)       | 0.001-0.1  |
    | d         | Filter order                      | 1 or 2     |

    Why these parameters are hard to tune per-signal

    *** NEW: Why BEADS Leaks in Dense-Peak Regions ***
    Three mechanistic failure modes (0.5-1 page within 2.2.1):

    (1) Sparsity assumption violation: The L1 penalty on x and its
        derivatives assumes peaks are sparse — most of x should be zero.
        When peaks are densely packed (metabolomics, complex mixtures),
        the composite signal is neither sparse nor has sparse derivatives.
        The L1 penalty over-shrinks peaks, and the surplus energy is
        attributed to the baseline.

    (2) Low-pass filter bandwidth mismatch: fc determines what counts
        as "baseline." When peak tails overlap and their valleys don't
        reach the true baseline, the sustained elevation appears
        low-frequency to the filter, causing it to interpret overlapping
        peak tails as baseline content. No single fc handles both sparse
        and dense regions.

    (3) Uniform regularization: Classical BEADS applies the same
        lambda_0, lambda_1, lambda_2 everywhere. Dense-peak regions need
        different regularization than sparse regions, but the algorithm
        has no mechanism to adapt spatially.

    Frame these as inherent to the BEADS formulation, not implementation
    bugs — this motivates the LBEADS-NET design choices in Chapter 3.

    Include figure: BEADS iteration diagram / block diagram
    Include table: parameter roles and typical ranges

  2.2.2: Other Classical Methods (~1.5–2 pages)
    - arPLS (Baek et al., 2015): asymmetric reweighted penalized least
      squares — iterative logistic-function reweighting
    - airPLS (Zhang et al., 2010): adaptive iteratively reweighted PLS
    - SNIP: Statistics-sensitive Non-linear Iterative Peak-clipping
    - AsLS (Eilers, 2003): Asymmetric Least Squares — foundational

    Position relative to BEADS: why BEADS is a good unrolling candidate
    (explicit optimization, clear per-iteration structure, interpretable params)

    *** NEW: BEADS vs arPLS failure mode contrast ***
    Key insight (one paragraph): arPLS has NO sparsity assumption — it
    fits a smooth curve below the data with adaptive weighting. In dense
    regions:
      - arPLS failure mode = "elevation" (baseline too high, but smooth)
      - BEADS failure mode = "leakage" (baseline follows peak shapes)
    Elevation is less damaging than leakage for quantification.
    This contrast motivates the LBEADS-NET design goal: learn to behave
    more like arPLS in dense regions and more like BEADS in sparse regions.

SECTION 2.3: Algorithm Unrolling (~4–5 pages)
  - Core idea: convert K iterations of an iterative algorithm into K
    trainable network layers, where algorithm parameters become learnable
  - LISTA lineage: Gregor & LeCun (2010) — the original unrolled ISTA
  - General framework: iterative algorithm -> computation graph -> backprop
  - Why it works: preserves algorithmic structure (interpretability) while
    allowing data-driven parameter learning (generalization)
  - Key advantages over black-box deep learning:
    . Interpretable intermediate representations
    . Fewer parameters (structured vs. free)
    . Convergence guarantees (in some cases)
  - Key unrolling architectures (brief survey):
    . LISTA (Gregor & LeCun, 2010): founding work
    . ALISTA (Liu et al., ICLR 2019): analytic weight initialization
    . ISTA-Net+ (Zhang & Ghanem, CVPR 2018): learned nonlinear transforms
    . ISTA-Net++ (You et al., IEEE TIP 2021): dynamic input-conditioned params
    . Hybrid ISTA (Zheng et al., TPAMI 2022): convergence guarantees
    . ADMM-Net (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2016): unrolled ADMM with learned
      piecewise-linear shrinkage
    . DeMUN (2025): comprehensive examination, intermediate supervision
  - How this applies to BEADS: each BEADS iteration becomes a BEADSLayer
    with learnable lambda_0, lambda_1, lambda_2, r per layer

  *** NEW: Baseline leakage as a known limitation of unrolled sparse recovery ***
  One paragraph: Gharbi, Chouzenoux, Pesquet & Duval (MLSP 2024,
  Signal Processing 2024) compared unrolled primal-dual, unrolled ISTA,
  and unrolled Half-Quadratic for 1D chromatographic signal restoration
  and found that unrolled HQ underestimates peak intensities (measured
  by TSNR). This confirms that baseline leakage is fundamental to the
  class of unrolled sparse-recovery networks, not specific to LBEADS-NET.
  DIRAS+ (Analytical Chemistry, 2025) explicitly called out baseline leakage
  as a fundamental limitation of end-to-end deep learning approaches.
  This validates the leakage problem addressed in this thesis.

SECTION 2.4: Neural Approaches to Baseline Correction (~2–3 pages)
  *** NEW SECTION — not in original blueprint ***

  2.4.1: Deep Learning for Chromatography / Spectroscopy
    - Kensert et al. (J. Chromatography A, 2021): 1D convolutional
      autoencoder trained on 190K simulated chromatograms. RMSE 1.094 mAU
      vs 2.074 for Savitzky-Golay. Demonstrates feasibility of synthetic-
      data-trained neural baseline correction.
    - CAE+ (Han et al., Sensors, 2024): convolutional autoencoder with
      comparison function for Raman spectroscopy. Peak preservation rates
      0.851-0.96. Found airPLS shows 2-3x higher error in peak regions
      vs CAE+ — quantifying the leakage problem.
    - ResNet+UNet (Chen et al., Analyst, 2022): hybrid architecture for
      Raman baseline correction, eliminating manual parameter adjustment.
    - 1dTrans (Zhao et al., 2025): first Transformer-based baseline
      estimator. Lower MAE than CNN, ResUNet, and classical methods.
    - RSPSSL (Hu et al., Light: Sci. & Appl., 2024): self-supervised via
      Raman Spectral GAN. 88% RMSE reduction, ~1900 spectra/second.

  2.4.2: Physics-Informed / Hybrid Approaches
    - DIRAS+ (Analytical Chemistry, 2025): CNN+XGBoost pipeline predicting
      optimal lambda per-spectrum, achieving 0.036 s/spectrum inference.
      Explicitly identifies baseline leakage as a fundamental limitation.
    - OP-airPLS (Analytical Chemistry, 2025): ML-predicted parameters for

  2.4.3: Positioning LBEADS-NET
    - Black-box neural methods: better generalization but no interpretability
    - Physics-informed hybrids: use ML to predict classical method params
    - LBEADS-NET: algorithm unrolling = the structure IS the model.
      Neither black-box nor external parameter prediction — parameters
      are learned jointly as part of the optimization architecture.
    - This is the unique positioning in the related work landscape.

SECTION 3.1: Problem Formulation (~1–2 pages)
  - Restate the signal decomposition: y = x + f + w
  - Goal: learn a mapping (y; Theta) -> (x_hat, f_hat) where Theta are
    trainable params
  - Design philosophy: unroll BEADS, don't replace it
  - Frame the challenge: the loss function must not only train the network
    to decompose signals, but actively counteract the sparsity-induced
    leakage inherent to the BEADS formulation (see Ch. 2.2.1)

SECTION 3.2: From BEADS to BEADSLayer (~4–5 pages)
  - How one BEADS iteration maps to one neural network layer
  - Learnable parameters per layer:
    . log_lambda_0, log_lambda_1, log_lambda_2: log-space for positivity
    . log_r: asymmetry ratio in log-space
    . step_size: learned gradient step size (ISTA variant)
    . output_gain: output scaling factor
    Total: 26 learned scalar parameters across 8 layers
  - BAfilt construction within the layer: banded Toeplitz structure,
    how the butterworth filter coefficients are computed
  - f-update step: banded linear system solve
    . Show the matrix equation: (B^T B + lambda_1 H^T H + lambda_2 I) f
      = B^T(y - x)
    . Banded structure -> efficient solve
  - x-update step: proximal/shrinkage operator
    . Soft-thresholding with asymmetric weights
    . The Theta weighting matrix and role of r
  - Include ALGORITHM BOX: single BEADSLayer forward pass pseudocode
  - Include FIGURE: single BEADSLayer architecture diagram

SECTION 3.3: The ISTA-Style Fast Variant (~2–3 pages)
  *** KEY ANALYTICAL CONTENT ***
  - Original design: CG-based variant (conjugate gradient for f-update)
  - Why CG failed: weak gradients through unrolled CG iterations,
    gradient vanishing over K layers, CG convergence criterion not
    differentiable
  - The fix: ISTA-style splitting for the f-update
    . Replace CG with a single-step proximal gradient update
    . Trade per-iteration accuracy for better gradient flow
  - Analytical argument: gradient flow comparison between CG and ISTA
    variants (this is examiner-grade content)
  - Design principle: simpler per-layer computation + more layers beats
    complex per-layer computation + fewer layers
  - Include FIGURE: gradient magnitude comparison across layers

SECTION 3.4: Full K-Layer Pipeline (~2–3 pages)
  - K=8 BEADSLayers stacked sequentially
  - Each layer has independent learnable parameters
  - Parameter specialization: early layers do coarse separation,
    later layers refine
  - Output: x_hat_K (peak estimate) and f_hat_K (baseline estimate)
  - Intermediate outputs at every layer: used for intermediate supervision
    and quality-scored stage selection
  - Include FIGURE: full K-layer pipeline diagram showing data flow

SECTION 3.5: Non-Learnable fc and the Autograd Boundary (~1–2 pages)
  *** ANOTHER KEY ANALYTICAL SECTION ***
  - fc (cutoff frequency) controls the BAfilt butterworth filter
  - BAfilt uses scipy.signal.butter -> NumPy/SciPy outside PyTorch autograd
  - Cannot backpropagate through scipy operations
  - Consequence: fc must be fixed as a hyperparameter (fc=0.006), not learned
  - This limits adaptivity to signals with different baseline frequency
    characteristics
  - Connection to leakage: fc directly controls the frequency boundary
    between "baseline" and "peaks" — a fixed fc means the model cannot
    adapt this boundary to dense vs sparse regions
  - Discussion: potential solutions (differentiable filter design,
    surrogate gradients) — connect to future work

SECTION 3.6: Multi-Stage Curriculum Training (~2–3 pages)
  - Motivation: composite loss is complex, direct optimization fails

  - Stage A (warmup, 5 epochs): MSE-only loss
    . Establishes basic peak/baseline separation
    . Prevents early divergence from conflicting loss gradients

  - Stage B (structured learning, 15 epochs):
    MSE + baseline supervision + asymmetric loss + orthogonality
    . Teaches correct separation behavior
    . Anti-leakage losses activate here: asymmetric baseline loss
      penalizes over-estimation 9x more than under-estimation,
      orthogonality forces mutual exclusivity of x and f

  - Stage C (refinement, 10 epochs): Full composite loss with all terms
    . Adds envelope constraint, frequency separation, all sparsity terms
    . Fine-tunes leakage suppression
    . Intermediate supervision enabled: loss at every unrolled layer
      with linearly increasing weights (0.1 -> 1.0)

  - Include TABLE: stage configuration (epochs, active loss terms, weights)

SECTION 3.7: Composite Loss Function (~3–4 pages)
  *** Frame as "engineering against leakage" ***

  Opening paragraph: The composite loss is designed with two objectives:
  (1) train accurate signal decomposition, and (2) actively suppress
  baseline leakage — the tendency of the sparsity-biased BEADS formulation
  to absorb peak energy into the baseline estimate. Each term targets a
  specific failure mode identified in Section 2.2.1.

  3.7.1: Reconstruction Loss
    - MSE: ||y - (x_hat + f_hat)||_2^2
    - Anchor term, alpha_mse = 1.0
    - Ensures decomposition reconstructs the original signal

  3.7.2: Sparsity Penalties
    - L1 on peaks: ||x_hat||_1 (alpha_l1 = 0.01)
    - TV on peaks: ||nabla x_hat||_1 (alpha_tv = 0.01)
    - Purpose: enforce sparse, sharp peak structure
    - Note the tension: these penalties are the CAUSE of leakage in
      dense regions (L1 pushes energy out of peaks), but are necessary
      for correct behavior in sparse regions. The weights are kept low
      to balance this tradeoff.

  3.7.3: Baseline Supervision (the critical fix, v4)
    - Masked MSE against ground-truth baseline in non-peak regions
    - alpha_baseline = 0.5
    - The masking strategy: only supervise where peaks are absent
    - This was the single most impactful anti-leakage intervention —
      directly tells the network what the baseline should look like

  3.7.4: Smoothness Penalty
    - ||nabla^2 f_hat||_2^2 on baseline (alpha_smooth = 0.2)
    - Enforces smooth, low-frequency baseline

  3.7.5: Non-Negativity Penalty + Softplus Constraint
    - Soft penalty: max(0, -x_hat)^2 (alpha_neg = 0.5)
    - Architectural constraint: softplus(x, beta=5) after output_gain
    - Why softplus over ReLU: smooth gradients, avoids dead neurons
    - beta=5 gives near-ReLU sharpness while maintaining differentiability
    - Connection to leakage: negative peak artifacts enable a mode where
      the network produces negative peaks to offset baseline over-estimation.
      Softplus eliminates this degenerate solution.

  3.7.6: Baseline Leakage Penalty
    - Correlation between peak residual and baseline (alpha_leakage = 0.3)
    - Detects when peak energy leaks into baseline estimate
    - Complements baseline supervision (which requires ground truth)

  3.7.7: Asymmetric Baseline Loss (NEW in v7, anti-leakage)
    - Penalizes over-estimation 9x more than under-estimation
    - alpha_asym_baseline = 1.0, asymmetry ratio alpha = 0.9
    - Rationale: baseline rising above true level = leakage = worse for
      quantification than slight under-estimation
    - Derived from AsLS family (Eilers, 2005) and arPLS (Baek et al., 2015)

  3.7.8: Element-Wise Orthogonality (evolved v6->v7, anti-leakage)
    - ||x_hat . f_hat||_1 (alpha_ortho = 0.1)
    - Enforces mutual exclusivity of peaks and baseline
    - Adapted from orthogonal NMF (Ding et al., KDD 2006; Pompili et al.,
      IEEE TKDE 2014)
    - Evolution: v6 used gradient-based penalty (mean(|diff1(f)|^2)) ->
      v7 switched to direct element-wise product, which is more effective

  3.7.9: Envelope Constraint (anti-leakage)
    - Baseline <= local signal minimum (alpha_envelope = 0.5)
    - Soft local minimum via temperature-scaled log-sum-exp:
      soft_min = -tau * logsumexp(-y_unfold / tau)
    - Prevents baseline from rising above what the signal supports
    - Window size = 51 (approximately 3x typical peak FWHM)

  3.7.10: Frequency Separation Loss
    - FFT-based: penalizes high-freq in baseline, low-freq in peaks
    - alpha_freq = 0.05
    - Enforces spectral role: baseline = low-frequency, peaks = broadband
    - Limitation: dense peaks themselves contain low-frequency energy,
      making perfect spectral separation impossible

  - Include TABLE: all loss terms, formulas, weights, which training
    stage activates them, and which failure mode each targets

SECTION 4.3: Evaluation Metrics (~2–3 pages)

  4.3.1: Standard Signal Decomposition Metrics
    - MSE(x, x_hat): mean squared error on peaks
    - MAE(x, x_hat): mean absolute error on peaks
    - Correlation(x, x_hat): Pearson correlation
    - MSE(f, f_hat): baseline estimation error
    - Area error: |integral(x) - integral(x_hat)| / integral(x)
    - Peak localization MAE (position error in samples)
    - Peak match rate (% of true peaks detected)
    - Define each precisely with formula

  4.3.2: Baseline Leakage Detection Metrics (NEW)
    *** These are critical for the thesis narrative ***

    Baseline Leakage Index (BLI):
      residual = f_pred - f_true
      leakage = ReLU(residual) * peak_mask   (only over-estimation in peaks)
      BLI = leakage.sum() / (peak_energy + eps)
    BLI > 0 indicates baseline absorbing peak energy. This metric
    directly measures the failure mode that motivated much of the
    loss function design.

    Peak-to-Baseline Error Ratio (PBER):
      PBER = RMSE(f_pred[peak_mask], f_true[peak_mask])
           / RMSE(f_pred[~peak_mask], f_true[~peak_mask])
    PBER > 1 indicates worse performance in peak regions = leakage.
    CAE+ (Han et al., 2024) found airPLS shows 2-3x higher error in
    peak vs baseline regions.

    Stratified Evaluation:
    - Classify regions as "sparse" (<1 peak per window) or "dense"
      (>3 overlapping peaks per window)
    - Report ALL metrics separately for sparse vs dense regions
    - Dense-region penalty = metric_dense / metric_sparse - 1
      -> quantifies degradation in the problematic regime

    Additional leakage-related metrics:
    - Peak-region RMSE: RMSE computed within +/- 3*sigma of each peak
    - Peak area preservation: |A_pred - A_true| / A_true * 100%
    - Peak height preservation: |H_pred - H_true| / H_true * 100%

SECTION 6.1: Baseline Leakage — Diagnosis and Mitigation (~3–4 pages)
  *** THE CORE DISCUSSION SECTION ***

  6.1.1: Root Cause Analysis (~1 page)
    - Restate the three failure modes from Ch. 2.2.1:
      (1) Sparsity assumption violation in dense-peak regions
      (2) Low-pass filter bandwidth mismatch — no single fc handles both
          sparse and dense regions
      (3) Uniform regularization — no spatial adaptivity
    - Frame as INHERENT to BEADS, not an implementation issue
    - Cite Gharbi et al. (2024) confirming this is a community-wide problem
    - Cite DIRAS+ (2025) identifying leakage as a fundamental DL limitation

  6.1.2: The Mitigation Journey — v3 to v7 (~1.5–2 pages)
    Tell the engineering story systematically:

    | Version | Mitigation                    | Outcome                         |
    |---------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------|
    | v3      | Sparsity losses (L1, TV)      | Made peaks sparser but didn't   |
    |         |                               | fix leakage — L1 is the CAUSE  |
    | v4      | Baseline supervision (masked  | Major improvement — baseline    |
    |         | MSE)                          | stops following peaks           |
    | v6      | Gradient-based orthogonality  | Modest — gradient penalty is    |
    |         |                               | indirect                       |
    | v7      | Asymmetric baseline loss (9:1)| Reduced over-estimation         |
    | v7      | Element-wise orthogonality    | Better mutual exclusivity       |
    | v7      | Envelope constraint           | Prevents baseline excursions    |
    |         |                               | above local minimum            |
    | v7      | Frequency separation (FFT)    | Enforces spectral roles, but    |
    |         |                               | dense peaks have low-freq too  |
    | v7      | Softplus constraint           | Eliminates negative-peak        |
    |         |                               | artifacts (a degenerate mode)  |

    For each: what the mitigation targeted, how it helped, and why it
    wasn't sufficient alone. Quantitative improvement where available
    (BLI reduction, PBER improvement).

  6.1.3: Fundamental Limits of Sparsity-Based Decomposition (~1 page)
    The thesis-level takeaway:
    - All mitigations are REGULARIZERS on top of a sparsity-biased
      formulation
    - The core BEADS optimization assumes sparsity; no amount of loss
      engineering fully overcomes this when the assumption is violated
    - The arPLS insight: methods without sparsity assumptions exhibit
      "elevation" (baseline too high but smooth) rather than "leakage"
      (baseline follows peak shapes) — a qualitatively different and
      less damaging failure mode
    - To truly fix the problem: need either (a) signal-adaptive sparsity
      that relaxes in dense regions, or (b) a non-sparsity-based
      decomposition (different paradigm)
    - This is not specific to BEADS or chromatography — any unrolled
      network inheriting L1-based sparsity will face this in dense regimes
    - This insight is a contribution to the algorithm unrolling community

SECTION 6.2: Training/Inference Length Mismatch (~1.5 pages)
  - Training: N = 1024, Inference: often N = 4096+
  - Distribution shift: longer signals have different spectral properties
  - Which components are length-sensitive (BAfilt, frequency separation)
    vs length-invariant (pointwise shrinkage, TV penalty)
  - fc=0.006 may be suboptimal at different N — different Nyquist-relative
    position
  - Potential fixes: variable-length training, sliding-window inference,
    convolutional architecture

SECTION 6.3: The CG Variant's Failure (~1.5 pages)
  *** ANALYTICAL CONTENT EXAMINERS VALUE ***
  - CG-based f-update: K_inner CG steps per layer x K_outer layers
  - Problem: gradient backprops through K_inner * K_outer sequential ops
    (e.g., 10 * 8 = 80 operations -> vanishing gradients)
  - CG convergence criterion is not differentiable
  - Lesson: not all iterative algorithms unroll equally well — the
    inner solver's gradient properties determine trainability
  - Design principle: simpler per-layer + more layers > complex per-layer
    + fewer layers
  - Connection to implicit differentiation literature
  - Include FIGURE: gradient magnitude per layer comparison

SECTION 6.4: Non-Learnable fc and the Autograd Boundary (~1 page)
  - fc controls butterworth filter cutoff in BAfilt
  - scipy.signal.butter -> outside PyTorch autograd
  - Cannot compute d(loss)/d(fc)
  - This limits adaptivity: signals with different baseline frequency
    characteristics all get the same fc
  - Connection to leakage: a learnable fc would allow the model to
    widen the baseline bandwidth in sparse regions and narrow it in
    dense regions, partially addressing the bandwidth mismatch failure mode
  - Potential fix: differentiable IIR filter design (future work)

SECTION 6.5: Softplus Approximation — Train/Inference Gap (~0.5–1 page)
  - softplus(x, beta=5) ~ ReLU but softplus(0) = ln(2)/5 ~ 0.139
  - Training: smooth gradients enable learning through zero-crossing
  - Inference: small positive bias in near-zero regions (~0.000016 min val)
  - General lesson for the unrolling community: learned proximal operators
    using approximate activations face train/test mismatch
  - This is a broader insight about differentiable relaxations

SECTION 7.1: Summary of Contributions (~1.5–2 pages)
  - LBEADS-NET: first algorithm-unrolled network for baseline correction
  - Demonstrated that BEADS iterations map naturally to trainable layers
    with emergent per-layer parameter specialization
  - Multi-stage curriculum training enables stable optimization of
    complex composite loss with 11 terms
  - Systematic diagnosis and mitigation of baseline leakage:
    . Identified three root causes (sparsity violation, bandwidth mismatch,
      uniform regularization)
    . Engineered loss-based countermeasures (asymmetric loss, orthogonality,
      envelope constraint)
    . Documented the mitigation journey across six development iterations
    . Established fundamental limits of sparsity-based decomposition in
      dense-peak regimes
  - Hybrid inference pipeline bridges learned and classical methods
  - Analytical insights: CG gradient collapse, fc non-learnability,
    softplus train/inference gap — valuable to the unrolling community

SECTION 7.2: Generalization Argument (~0.5 pages)
  - The unrolling methodology is not specific to BEADS
  - Any iterative signal decomposition algorithm with clear per-iteration
    structure is a candidate for unrolling
  - LBEADS-NET demonstrates the template: identify parameters -> make
    learnable -> design loss -> curriculum training
  - The leakage analysis generalizes too: any unrolled L1-based method
    will face similar challenges in dense regimes

SECTION 7.3: Future Directions (~2–2.5 pages)
  *** Enriched with concrete directions from leakage research ***

  7.3.1: Learned Proximal Operators
    - Replace hand-designed soft-thresholding with small learned CNN
    - Residual structure: output = soft_threshold(x) + CNN(x)
    - Follows ISTA-Net+ (Zhang & Ghanem, CVPR 2018)
    - Key advantage for leakage: can learn to NOT shrink in dense regions
    - Convergence guarantees: Hybrid ISTA (Zheng et al., TPAMI 2022) proves
      convergence if learned component satisfies contractivity condition

  7.3.2: Signal-Adaptive Parameter Prediction
    - Small encoder predicting per-signal lambda adjustments
    - Follows ISTA-Net++ (You et al., IEEE TIP 2021)
    - DIRAS+ uses CNN+XGBoost for per-spectrum lambda prediction
    - Most principled fix for leakage: dense regions get lower lambda_0
      automatically — the network learns when to relax sparsity

  7.3.3: Making fc Differentiable
    - Differentiable IIR filter design: parameterize frequency response
      directly in PyTorch
    - Replace butterworth with learnable 1D convolution kernels
    - Signal-adaptive fc via meta-learning / hypernetwork
    - Would allow the model to adapt the baseline/peak frequency boundary

  7.3.4: ADMM Reformulation
    - Unroll ADMM instead of MM/ISTA
    - ADMM variable splitting: peaks and baseline as separate auxiliary vars
    - ADMM-Net (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2016): piecewise-linear shrinkage
      more flexible than soft-thresholding
    - Natural separation between data fidelity and regularization

  7.3.5: Deep Equilibrium Model (DEQ)
    - Convert LBEADS-NET to DEQ: iterate until convergence per signal
    - Dense-peak signals that need more iterations get them automatically
    - O(1) memory via implicit differentiation
    - MsDC-DEQ-Net (Yu & Dansereau, 2024), GUDL (2025)
    - Bai, Kolter & Koltun (NeurIPS 2019)

  7.3.6: Alternative Architectures
    - Conformer (Gulati et al., 2020): convolution + self-attention captures
      both local peak shapes and global baseline trends
    - Wave-U-Net (Stoller et al., 2018): difference output layer enforces
      energy conservation (peaks = signal - baseline)
    - WaveNet-style dilated convolutions: large receptive field preserving
      sample-level resolution. Learnable dilation won MIT RF Challenge
      (ICASSP 2024).
    - Mamba / SSMs (Gu & Dao, 2023): linear O(N) with data-dependent
      selection. No paper has applied Mamba to chromatography baseline
      correction — novel direction.

  7.3.7: Extension to Other Signal Types
    - Raman spectroscopy baseline correction
    - Mass spectrometry
    - ECG baseline wander correction
    - Cross-domain generalization
